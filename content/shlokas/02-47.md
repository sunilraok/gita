# BG 2.47 — Your right is to the effort, not the fruit

## Sanskrit

> कर्मण्येवाधिकारस्ते मा फलेषु कदाचन ।
> मा कर्मफलहेतुर्भूर्मा ते सङ्गोऽस्त्वकर्मणि ॥ २.४७ ॥

*karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana*
*mā karma-phala-hetur bhūr mā te saṅgo 'stv akarmaṇi*

## Meaning

You have a right to your actions, but never to their results. Don't let the
expected reward be your reason for acting — and don't let that become an excuse
for doing nothing either.

**Word by word:** *karmaṇi* — in action · *eva* — only · *adhikāraḥ* — right ·
*te* — your · *mā phaleṣu* — not in the fruits · *kadācana* — ever ·
*mā karma-phala-hetuḥ bhūḥ* — do not be motivated by the fruit of action ·
*mā te saṅgaḥ astu* — let there be no attachment for you · *akarmaṇi* — to inaction.

*Public-domain rendering (Edwin Arnold, 1885):* "Let right deeds be thy motive,
not the fruit which comes from them."

## Why it matters

The verse has **four parts**, and people usually remember only the first:

1. Act — that is in your hands.
2. The result is not solely in your hands.
3. So don't make the reward your only motive.
4. **But don't use that as a reason to stop acting.**

It removes the anxiety of outcomes *without* removing ambition or accountability.

## Life lenses

### 👪 Family
A parent helps a child prepare for exams, cooks the meals, keeps the home calm —
and then lets go of the rank. Loving action is the parent's part; the child's
journey is the child's. Families that praise *effort* ("you worked so steadily")
rather than only *marks* raise children who keep trying after a setback.

### 🎓 Student
Your job is the hours of honest study, the practice papers, the doubts you clear
with your teacher. The exam paper, the evaluator, the cut-off — these are not
fully in your control. Students who study with this attitude sleep better the
night before an exam and perform better because fear no longer eats their focus.

### 💼 Workplace
You own the quality of the proposal, the code, the pitch. The client's decision,
the market, the promotion committee are factors outside you. Measure yourself on
what you control (preparation, integrity, follow-through). When a deal is lost,
review the *process*, not your self-worth — then pick up the next task. The last
line also warns against the opposite trap: "results aren't mine, so why bother?"
is not detachment, it is disengagement.

## Story — The bird's eye *(Mahabharata, Adi Parva)*

Guru Drona placed a wooden bird on a tree and asked his students to aim at its
eye. Before each one shot, he asked, "What do you see?" One saw the tree, the
branches and the bird; another saw the sky and the leaves. Drona told each to put
down the bow. Arjuna answered, "I see only the bird's eye." "Shoot," said Drona —
and Arjuna struck the mark.

**Connection:** Arjuna's attention was entirely on the act in front of him, not
on the applause, not on his rivals, not on the prize. Full presence in the action
is exactly what 2.47 asks for.

### Modern vignette *(illustrative)*
Ravi, a team lead, lost a big client pitch after three weeks of late nights. For
two days he stayed bitter. Then he held a short review with his team: "What did
*we* control? What would we do differently?" They found two real gaps and fixed
them. The next pitch, for a different client, won — but Ravi told the team the
thing he was proudest of was how they had handled the loss.

## Common misreading
"Don't care about results." — **No.** Plan for results, aim high, measure
outcomes. The verse asks you not to be *attached* to them or *paralysed* by them.

## Try this today
Before one important task, write two lists: **"In my hands"** and **"Not in my
hands."** Put your full energy into the first list only.
