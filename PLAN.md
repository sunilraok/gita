# Gita for Everyday Life — Project Plan

> *"yogaḥ karmasu kauśalam"* — Yoga is skill in action. (BG 2.50)

## 1. Vision

Build a curated, beautifully illustrated collection of the **most widely acclaimed
shlokas of the Bhagavad Gita that are useful in day-to-day life**. Each shloka is
presented with:

1. The original Sanskrit (Devanagari) and a romanised transliteration (IAST)
2. A plain-language meaning and a short word-by-word gloss
3. **Life lenses** — how it applies to *families*, *students*, *corporate employees
   / leaders*, and (where it fits) *seniors* and *homemakers*
4. **A story** — from the Mahabharata, the Puranas, history, or a clearly labelled
   modern illustrative vignette
5. **A graphic** — a shloka card, and for key ideas an explanatory diagram
6. A one-line **daily practice** ("Try this today")

The tone is reverent but practical: faithful to the text, free of sectarian
bias, and understandable by a 15-year-old.

## 3. How we choose the shlokas

Each candidate verse gets a simple score:

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| **Acclaim** — appears in N of ~10 surveyed "best shlokas" lists, traditional compilations (Gita Dhyānam, Chatuḥślokī Gita 10.8–11, Gita Sāra) and famous public quotations | 40% |
| **Everyday usefulness** — speaks to stress, work, relationships, learning, habits, grief, decisions | 40% |
| **Breadth** — covers a theme or chapter not already covered | 20% |

Verses that are mainly metaphysical (e.g. cosmology of chapter 11) are included
only if they carry a clear practical message.

## 5. Template for each shloka entry

See a fully worked example: [`content/shlokas/03-2.47.yaml`](content/shlokas/03-2.47.yaml).

```
Title + theme tag + chapter/verse
Sanskrit (Devanagari) | Transliteration (IAST)
Meaning (2–3 lines, our words) | Word-by-word gloss
Public-domain translation (Arnold 1885) for flavour
Life lenses:  👪 Family · 🎓 Student · 💼 Workplace · (🌿 Seniors)
Story  — with a source label:
          [Mahabharata] [Purana] [Folk tradition] [History] [Illustrative]
Common misreading (what this verse does NOT say)
Try this today (one small action)
Related verses
Sources
```

Story labels keep us honest: popular tales like *Vidura's wife offering banana
peels* are folk tradition, not in the critical edition, and are marked so.

**Typography:** *Tiro Devanagari Sanskrit* or *Noto Serif Devanagari* for

**Motifs (drawn as simple vector line-art, no copyrighted images):** peacock
feather, bansuri (flute), lotus, chariot wheel (dharma chakra), conch (śaṅkha),
diya lamp, manuscript border.

## 10. Quality guardrails

- **Textual accuracy:** Sanskrit and transliteration are imported programmatically
  from the public-domain dataset, never retyped by hand.
- **Faithful meaning:** each meaning is checked against at least two independent
  translations; where traditions differ (e.g. 18.66), we say so briefly.
- **No overreach:** we don't claim the Gita "proves" management theories; the
  applications are *inspired by* the verse.
- **Honest stories:** every story carries a source label; fictional vignettes are
  marked as illustrative.
- **Respectful imagery:** no caricatures of deities; line-art motifs over
  figurative depictions unless reviewed.
- **Attribution:** a Sources section on every entry and a site-wide credits page.

## 12. What is built (phase 1)

- `content/shlokas/*.yaml`: 21 bilingual entries (title, key message, meaning, three life lenses, story with source label, common misreading, daily practice, related verses).
- `scripts/build.py`: merges the entries with the public-domain Sanskrit text, generates Kannada script from the Devanagari by code, and writes `site/data/shlokas.js`.
- `site/`: the website (home with shloka of the day, theme filters, 21 shloka pages, about page, settings), plus `site/illustrations.js`, which draws the 21 illustrations.
- `.github/workflows/pages.yml`: deploys `site/` to GitHub Pages on pushes to `main`.
